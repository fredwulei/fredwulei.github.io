About

I've spent the last decade or so on backend and platform work: designing distributed architectures, untangling long-lived legacy systems, and more recently applying LLMs to problems where correctness and evaluation actually matter.

These days my work splits between two things. One is core platform architecture — microservices, event sourcing, API contracts, and data modeling that has to hold up under billions of transactions a year and an unreasonable amount of country-by-country variation. The other is AI systems — multi-agent orchestration, enterprise knowledge retrieval, secure tool integration, and the evaluation harnesses that keep them honest.

Outside of work I mostly build things. Neverland AI is my personal AI platform — agents over my own knowledge base, with skills, artifacts and a chat UI I use every day. Workland is that same instinct pointed at business: a B2B AI platform where a team defines a process and an assistant works the records alongside them. Then there are the toys — Habitually, an iOS habit tracker, and Second Serve, a tennis career simulation.

Away from the keyboard it's tennis, sailing, climbing and skiing, as many national parks as I can get to, a stack of books, and a lot of classical music.

Workland

Every small business already has a process — it's in a spreadsheet, a chat thread, and one person's head. Workland writes it down once: a workflow is the steps it goes through, and every run of it is a case, standing on one step, collecting what that step asks for, and moving on. Then point the assistant at it — it works the same steps, by the same rules, on the same record, hands on the work rather than a chatbot beside it.
